## Mathematical and scope repairs

1. The `1/m` boundary is a closed form for this constructed one-hot-value fixture without rotation. With RoPE, use the computed weighted cosines and display the correction. Ties are not a strict recall success even when argmax chooses the first class.
2. The general exact identity does not imply this particular recall condition for arbitrary keys, signed affinities, targets, or values. Keep the fixture scope attached to the claim.
3. The Welch bound forces pairwise overlap, not necessarily a wrong class argmax. Replace the brief's implication of inevitable classification failure with a measurable cross-talk statement. Show actual recall separately.
4. A random thresholded lift is a teaching simplification. It neither reproduces BDH's learned encoder nor validates the paper's asymptotic theorem empirically.
5. The broad phrase that this boundary is the mechanism behind production models' recall weakness is unsupported. Use an illustrative mechanism link, not a causal model diagnosis.
6. Random-run capacity at 90% depends on seed, value classes, sampling grid, and the definition of capacity. Display those definitions; do not assert the exploratory capacities in the brief.
7. The conformance script tests attention products and the change of frame only. It is not a trained-model reproduction and excludes LayerNorm and the full BDH block.
8. Automated scripts are independent numerical checks, not evidence that the human team independently verified the math. Disclosure must say the team still needs to review and defend it.
9. Sealed-box fault identification is relative to an explicitly enumerated finite hypothesis class, action menu, and budget. It does not identify proprietary internals.
